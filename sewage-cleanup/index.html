Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. Across Saratoga Springs, Saratoga Springs Restoration answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

Sewage damage is classified as Category 3 (black water), the most contaminated and dangerous category of water damage. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. We work to the IICRC S500 standard that governs Category 3 losses, with the goal of returning your property to a condition safe to occupy.

Here is what the work covers: standing sewage is pumped out, contaminated water is extracted from the structure, porous materials the sewage touched (drywall, insulation, carpet and padding) are torn out and disposed of properly, hard surfaces are disinfected thoroughly, antimicrobials are applied, and the structure is dried so mold does not follow. To finish, thermal foggers and hydroxyl generators of industrial grade deodorize the space so the sewage smell does not linger.

When to Call for Sewage Cleanup in Saratoga Springs

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Several fixtures backing up at once instead of only one
  • Running a sink or washer and hearing a toilet or nearby drain gurgle
  • Drain openings or basement slabs coated with visible sewage residue
  • Toilet water with waste in it soaking into carpet or running across the floor
  • Soggy, smelly ground over the sewer line or leakage at the cleanout cap
  • Foul-smelling moisture showing up on flooring or along the walls
  • Strong sewage or rotten egg odors coming from drains or lower levels

Frequently Asked Questions About Sewage Cleanup

A blocked sewer line on the property is the most common starting point. Grease buildup, wipes and other items that should not be flushed, and tree roots growing into the pipe are the usual causes. Older lines can also crack or collapse, and heavy rain can overload the municipal sewer and push sewage back through floor drains. A camera run through the line after cleanup identifies the actual cause.
What each item is made of decides that. Hard, non-porous items such as metal, glass, sealed tile and solid plastic can usually be cleaned and disinfected. Upholstery, mattresses, carpet with its pad, insulation and drywall soak up sewage and cannot be made safe, so they are thrown out. We document what is removed so you have a record for your insurance claim.
Nobody, pets included, should walk through the area the sewage reached. Stop using sinks, toilets and appliances that drain, since more water makes the backup worse. Turn off power and the HVAC system to the affected area only if you can do so without touching contaminated water. Do not try to clean it yourself, and wash thoroughly if you touched anything.
In most cases our advice is to relocate temporarily while the cleanup happens, above all when contamination is widespread. Airborne pathogens and the chemicals used for decontamination can both pose health risks. After seeing your situation firsthand, our team tells you what makes sense.
Standard homeowner's insurance often does NOT cover sewage backup unless you have a specific sewage or water backup endorsement. The coverage usually runs only $50 to $100 per year and gives critical protection, so our strong recommendation is to add it. Check your policy or ask your agent.

Why Hire a Professional for Sewage Cleanup?

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. Hepatitis, gastrointestinal disease and infections of the skin or respiratory system are among the serious illnesses these pathogens cause.

Contaminated materials count as biohazard waste, and disposing of them properly is part of a professional cleanup. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.

Sewage Cleanup Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • In an emergency a plumber needs the cleanout, so learn where yours is and keep it in good shape
  • Protect against municipal sewer backups by having a backwater valve fitted where your line connects
  • Have your sewer line inspected with a camera every 2 to 3 years to catch potential problems
  • Never pour grease, oil or food waste down your drains, since these cause blockages
  • Fit a strainer over each sink and shower drain so hair and debris stay out of the pipes
  • A drain that is slowing down should be dealt with early, while it is still partly open
