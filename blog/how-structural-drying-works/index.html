The Science of Structural Drying in Saratoga Springs, NY

Home / Blog / Understanding Professional Structural Drying in Saratoga Springs, NY

Few Saratoga Springs, NY homeowners know much about structural drying, even though the rest of a restoration job depends on it. Pumping out the standing water still leaves a great deal of moisture inside the insulation, concrete, subfloor, wood framing, and drywall. Left in place, that moisture ends in odors, mold, and materials that break down. Below is a plain look at the science professionals rely on, and at why their results are much better than what DIY drying achieves.

Psychrometry: The Science Behind Drying

The field behind professional drying is called psychrometry, which examines how mixtures of gas and vapor behave physically and thermodynamically. Put simply, it is about the way air movement, relative humidity, and temperature work together to pull water from wet materials. Every job is different, so technicians with IICRC certification calculate the setup psychrometrically, from air mover placement and target humidity and temperature to the number and kind of dehumidifiers.

Commercial Drying Equipment

The equipment used for professional structural drying operates on an entirely different scale than consumer products. LGR (Low-Grain Refrigerant) dehumidifiers can remove 15 to 30 or more pints of moisture per hour from the air, compared to 2 to 3 pints per hour for a residential unit. Evaporation is sped up by air movers that deliver a directed, high-velocity stream of 2,500 CFM or more across wet surfaces. For special cases such as drying in cold weather or very heavy moisture loads, crews turn to desiccant dehumidifiers. This equipment represents tens of thousands of dollars in investment and requires specific training to deploy effectively.

The Drying Process Step by Step

A professional drying job is done in a set order. First, a thorough moisture assessment maps all affected areas using moisture meters and thermal imaging. This map guides equipment placement and establishes baseline moisture readings. Dehumidifiers and air movers are positioned according to psychrometric calculations to create the best conditions for evaporation and moisture removal. Sometimes the space is heated so evaporation goes faster. Daily visits follow, with readings at each monitoring point, equipment adjustments where needed, and a record of how drying is going. The job is not finished until every wet material tests at an acceptable level against a dry reference standard.

How Many Days Will Drying Take?

With the right commercial equipment, a typical home dries in 3 to 5 days. Several things set the schedule: the amount and kind of equipment in use, the way the building was built, surrounding conditions, the materials involved, with concrete drying much more slowly than drywall, and the quantity of water present. Typical figures are 5 to 7 days or more for concrete, 3 to 4 days for wood framing, and 2 to 3 days for standard drywall. Your technician will provide a projected timeline based on initial moisture readings and adjust it as drying progresses.

Why Daily Readings Matter

Without a daily check, drying is only a guess. Nobody can say that materials are drying, keeping to the expected rate, or safely dry unless a calibrated instrument has measured them. A wall or floor may seem dry under your hand and still be dangerously wet deeper inside. Verifying it takes a moisture meter that has been calibrated. There is a paperwork benefit too: documented drying proves to an insurance company that the job met industry protocol, and that backs up your claim and your home's value.
