Warning Signs for Water Extraction

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Stairwells or window wells that fill up and spill water inside
  • Ceilings, light fixtures or walls that are actively leaking water
  • An appliance, HVAC unit or pipe that is leaking right now
  • A utility area, mechanical room or crawl space with water in it
  • Water squeezing up between floorboards, planks or tiles when you walk
  • Standing water on any floor surface, whatever the source
  • Storm water collecting in the lowest level of the home

What a Professional Brings to Water Extraction

Both the harm done to your home and the restoration bill depend directly on quick, thorough extraction. Truck-mounted extractors move water at hundreds of times the rate of consumer tools, and a job that could cost you a full day with a shop vac is done by our machines in minutes. Speed matters for more than comfort: every extra hour of water sitting against building materials can mean thousands of dollars more in damage.

Professional extraction also addresses moisture that you cannot see or reach with consumer tools. It follows plumbing lines and electrical conduits, slips between flooring layers, climbs drywall by wicking and is drawn into carpet pad through capillary action. Our specialty extraction tools and techniques target these hidden moisture deposits, removing significantly more water than surface extraction alone. The payoff is a shorter drying period, lower equipment cost and, most important of all, less risk of mold growing unseen in trapped moisture.

Water Extraction Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Look beneath sinks, behind toilets and beside appliances every so often to catch leaks early
  • A sump pump that has not been checked may fail in a storm, so test it ahead of wet weather
  • Raise anything you store in the basement a few inches above the floor
  • Save (510) 447-5252 in your contacts now, and you can reach us at once when something floods
  • Maintain appliance supply lines and replace hoses that show wear or age
  • Leak-sensing shutoff valves can cut off the water on their own when something fails
