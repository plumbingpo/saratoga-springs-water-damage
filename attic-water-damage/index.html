Attic Water Damage Help in Saratoga Springs

Water reaches an attic from two directions. From above, rain enters through failed flashing, worn shingles, or the seals at chimneys and vents. From below, household moisture rises and condenses on the cold roof sheathing if attic ventilation is poor. In both cases, almost nobody climbs up to check. In Saratoga Springs, we inspect the attic, track down the source, and get it dry.

Most attic jobs center on wet insulation. Heating and cooling bills go up because cellulose and fiberglass alike give up R-value when wet. The bigger problem is that for weeks, saturated insulation holds moisture on the drywall of the ceiling and on the framing. Saturated material comes out, air movers and dehumidifiers dry the wood, and any mold on the sheathing or rafters gets treated.

Warning Signs

  • Black or white fuzzy growth on the sheathing, often heaviest near the eaves
  • Daylight showing around a vent pipe, chimney, or along a roof valley
  • Closets on the top floor, or the attic, that smell musty
  • Paint that peels or bubbles on the ceiling of an upstairs room
  • Rust, water marks or drips on ductwork and metal straps in the attic
  • Rusty nail tips poking through the sheathing, sometimes with water beads on them
Our Process

Our Attic Water Damage Process

1

Inspect and Find the Source

We check the roof deck, flashing points, vents and exhaust ducts to learn whether the water is a leak, condensation, or both. Thermal imaging and moisture meters show how far it reached.

2

Remove Wet Insulation

We bag and remove insulation that is moldy or saturated, which lets the drywall and wood beneath it dry. Rooms under the attic are protected, and the route in and out stays contained.

3

Dry the Wood

With dehumidifiers and air movers, the sheathing, joists and rafters return to normal moisture levels. An antimicrobial goes on after any visible mold has been cleaned from the wood.

4

Fix the Cause

You are shown the ventilation or roof problem to be corrected, and our findings can go to your roofer. Once the attic stays dry, new insulation goes in to restore the R-value.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

First storm

Insulation Takes It

Rain that gets past the roof lands in the insulation, which absorbs it. With no sign inside the house, nobody notices the leak and the roof stays unrepaired.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold Can Start

Mold does very well on wet paper facing and damp lumber when the attic is warm and the air is still. It can start on the back side of the ceiling drywall and on the sheathing.

A few weeks on

Ceiling Stains Appear

Once storm after storm has saturated the insulation, the ceiling starts getting wet. In the room underneath, brown rings show up and the drywall may begin sagging.

Months later

Rot Sets In

Roofing nails loosen as the sheathing delaminates. Mold spreads across large sections of the roof deck, and the rafters remain damp enough to decay.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

A Falling Ceiling

Saturated insulation is heavy. The ceiling drywall under it weakens where it is fastened, and it can give way suddenly and bring the wet insulation along.

Water Near Electrical

Cable runs, junction boxes and recessed light housings are all found in attics. When they get wet there is a risk of shock and fire, so keep out until the power has been checked.

Lost Insulation Value

Compressed, damp insulation does very little insulating, and it may not recover after it dries. The rooms beneath that section get colder or hotter, and you pay more for energy.

Leaks From Attic Equipment

Many attics hold an air handler, a drain pan and condensate lines. For weeks an overflowing pan or blocked drain can drip into insulation, and there is no storm to tip you off.
